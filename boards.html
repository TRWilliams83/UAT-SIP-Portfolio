DMF Objective 1

Using digital and physical tools, engineer and build prototypes that technology patents are then applied for.

Related courses: DBM100, DBM150, DBM215, TCH410, TCH270, TCH200

Perpetual Planter

The Perpetual Planter is an original reusable insert that converts a standard planter into a passive water reservoir. Fusion 360 was used to create and revise the internal geometry, define dimensions, and evaluate the part through structural simulation before physical production.

The design addresses overwatering, underwatering, and root rot while allowing the user to retain an existing planter. Its documented function, construction, revisions, dimensions, and alternative configurations support continued patent-development work.

  • Original functional product concept
  • CAD development and multiple revisions
  • Structural simulation and dimensional documentation
  • Patent proposal and product variations
View Perpetual Planter Project

Project C2: Capillary Canopy

Project C2 is a modular terrarium lid engineered to manage moisture and enclosure conditions through passive geometry. The system combines condensation routing, vent control, sealed fitment, and optional water-transfer features in a set of 3D-printed components.

The project progressed from an innovation claim through CAD revisions, physical prototypes, environmental testing, technical drawings, and a final SIP brief. This work documents the construction, operation, variations, and intended function of the invention.

  • Original innovation claim
  • CAD and additive-manufacturing development
  • Multiple tested physical revisions
  • Technical drawings and final SIP documentation
View Project C2

DMF Objective 2

Build physical products while demonstrating technique and safety competency across commonly accepted prototyping devices, maker tools, and techniques.

Related courses: DBM150, DBM215, TCH410, TCH270, TCH200

Screw School

Screw School demonstrates the complete transition from a digital model to a manufactured storage product. Fusion 360 was used to create the tray geometry, screw openings, stacking pegs, labels, and connection features. The parts were sliced, printed, assembled, and tested with actual screws.

Revisions corrected disconnected pegs, failed text, and slot geometry that did not guide screws properly. This required competent and safe use of CAD, slicing software, additive-manufacturing equipment, materials, and physical assembly techniques.

  • Fusion 360 design and revision
  • Bambu Studio print preparation
  • Multiple manufactured prototypes
  • Physical assembly and fit testing
View Screw Stack Project

Four-Gear Mechanism

The Four-Gear Mechanism is a working mechanical assembly made from separate 12-, 24-, 36-, and 48-tooth gears installed on a custom mounting plate. Center distances were calculated from the gear radii so the teeth would engage correctly.

The finished assembly was manufactured, assembled, and tested as a connected mechanism with an overall 1:4 relationship. The process required accurate CAD construction, mechanical spacing, component organization, joint setup, and motion testing.

  • Four functional gear components
  • Calculated mounting distances
  • Custom supporting base plate
  • Physical assembly and motion testing
View Four-Gear Project

DMF Objective 3

Produce products that balance form and function while reflecting current and future trends in design and human factors.

Related courses: DBM100, DBM215, TCH410, TCH270, TCH200, TCH100

Modular Launch Glider

The Modular Launch Glider balances aerodynamic form with the manufacturing and assembly requirements of a 3D-printed aircraft. Its wing shape, stabilizers, tail, connector, and launcher interface support flight while remaining practical to manufacture and assemble.

The approximately 19-inch wingspan, modular construction, alignment features, and launcher were developed around performance, handling, transportation, repair, and user interaction.

  • Aerodynamic form with printable construction
  • Modular wing, tail, and connector components
  • Alignment and launcher features
  • Assembly and handling considerations
View Modular Launch Glider Project

Ergonomic Mouse

The Ergonomic Mouse is a functional computer-mouse design developed around a more natural hand position, shaped grip surfaces, and integrated wrist support. Its custom enclosure is intended to improve comfort during extended use while maintaining a clean, practical form.

The enclosure still had to fit the electronic components and provide access to the buttons, scroll wheel, optical sensor, and grip surfaces. Sketches and CAD revisions were used to examine comfort, natural hand position, wrist support, hardware fit, appearance, and usability.

  • Designed around required mouse components
  • Grip, hand-position, and wrist-support considerations
  • Ergonomic grip and integrated wrist support
  • Balanced comfort, visual identity, and hardware function
View Ergonomic Mouse Project

DMF Objective 4

Demonstrate the ability to evaluate material and build-technique options during the creation of products and their prototypes.

Related courses: DBM150, DBM215, TCH410, TCH270, TCH200

Modular Launch Glider

The Modular Launch Glider required evaluating how the aircraft could be divided, printed, assembled, and reinforced while remaining light enough to fly. Development considered print orientation, joining methods, wall thickness, impact areas, strength, and weight.

Simulation and prototype revisions identified concentrated stress and likely failure areas. The launcher was also developed to align with the glider, contact the nose correctly, and absorb force during launch.

  • Modular and assembled construction comparison
  • Stress and failure-area evaluation
  • Additive-manufacturing constraints
  • Strength, weight, and printability balance
View Modular Launch Glider Project

Screw School

Screw School involved comparing PLA and PETG, evaluating 3D printing and CNC production, and repeatedly changing component geometry. The selected material needed enough flexibility to accept screws without cracking while remaining durable during repeated use.

Manufacturing tests showed that font size, nozzle capability, peg attachment, fillet depth, slot dimensions, and screw spacing directly affected the finished product. Prototype failures were used to guide material and manufacturing changes.

  • PLA and PETG comparison
  • 3D-printing and CNC-process evaluation
  • Slot, fillet, peg, and label revisions
  • Manufacturing results used to guide changes
View Screw Stack Project

DMF Objective 5

Demonstrate the ability to effectively implement embedded systems and fundamental electronics into product builds.

Related courses: RBT173, RBT211, TCH410, TCH270

Ergonomic Mouse

The Ergonomic Mouse integrates a wireless mouse electronics kit into a custom-designed and 3D-printed ergonomic enclosure. The enclosure was developed around the circuit board, optical sensor, scroll wheel, switches, battery connection, and wireless receiver.

Internal clearances, mounting points, sensor openings, button movement, and component access must align with the electronic system for the product to function.

  • Wireless mouse electronics kit
  • Internal component support and clearance
  • Sensor, switches, wheel, power, and circuit-board integration
  • Custom-manufactured electronics enclosure

Status: Final assembly in progress.

View Ergonomic Mouse Project

Gaia Terrarium Monitor

The Gaia Terrarium Monitor is a compact terrarium-monitoring node that combines an ESP32-WROOM-32, DS18B20 digital temperature sensor, and SSD1306 OLED display.

The ESP32 receives and processes sensor data before displaying the current temperature on the OLED. The system demonstrates sensor input, microcontroller processing, display output, power distribution, wiring, and integration into a custom Fusion 360 enclosure.

  • ESP32-WROOM-32 microcontroller
  • DS18B20 digital temperature sensor
  • SSD1306 I2C OLED display
  • USB power and custom enclosure integration

Status: Working prototype.

DMF Objective 6

Place prototypes and builds within the Agile and MVP development frameworks.

Related courses: DBM215, DBM360

Perpetual Planter

The Perpetual Planter began as a focused passive MVP that converted a standard planter into a reservoir. Multiple mesh-disc versions, dimensional revisions, and simulations were then used to improve the product without expanding the first prototype beyond its core purpose.

Water-level sensing, soil-moisture monitoring, temperature monitoring, LEDs, and multi-pot communication were identified as later development possibilities rather than requirements for the initial functional build.

  • Focused passive MVP
  • Multiple compared design iterations
  • Simulation used to identify overengineering
  • Essential and future features separated
View Perpetual Planter Project

Project C2: Capillary Canopy

Project C2 followed a documented design-build-test-revise process across four major prototype versions. Each revision responded to evidence from fitment, condensation, evaporation, ventilation, humidity, and water-transfer testing.

The MVP focused on proving that a fitted 3D-printed lid could influence the terrarium environment through passive geometry. Features were simplified, revised, or removed when testing showed that they did not perform as expected.

  • Repeated prototype revisions
  • Testing results used to prioritize changes
  • Failed features revised or removed
  • Milestones, results, and next steps documented
View Project C2

The working prototype combines sensor input, microcontroller processing, local display output, USB power, and a custom-designed enclosure into a compact monitoring product.
