Project C2: Boards Objectives

How Project C2 supports the Digital Maker and Fabrication program objectives.

Overview

This page maps Project C2: Capillary Canopy to the Digital Maker and Fabrication program objectives. The project demonstrates applied design, additive manufacturing, iterative prototyping, testing, and product development through the creation of passive terrarium lid systems intended to manage microclimates through geometry rather than external equipment.

Objective 1

[Insert official UAT objective text here]

Project C2 demonstrates this objective through the design and development of biome-specific lid systems intended to control moisture behavior and enclosure conditions using passive mechanisms. Instead of relying on pumps, lights, or external controls, the project treats the lid itself as the environmental system.

  • Designed multiple lid revisions for different environmental behavior
  • Built around passive control rather than external equipment
  • Focused on turning a simple lid into a functional system

Objective 2

[Insert official UAT objective text here]

The project supports this objective through iterative CAD modeling in Fusion 360, where fitment, vent placement, channel geometry, and internal part relationships were revised based on real testing results. Each revision was informed by physical performance rather than just screen-based design.

  • Modeled parts and revisions in Fusion 360
  • Adjusted spacing, vent alignment, and cap fit across versions
  • Used repeated design-test-revise cycles

Objective 3

[Insert official UAT objective text here]

Project C2 demonstrates additive fabrication through the production of multiple functional prototypes using a Bambu P1P. Each print was used to evaluate fit, assembly, and environmental behavior, turning digital models into real testable parts.

  • Printed multiple prototype versions
  • Used physical prints for fitment and performance checks
  • Translated CAD models into working test parts

Objective 4

[Insert official UAT objective text here]

The project demonstrates testing and validation by documenting moisture behavior, airflow limitations, condensation patterns, fitment consistency, and wick performance across prototype revisions. Failure points were treated as useful design feedback rather than discarded mistakes.

  • Observed moisture behavior over time
  • Tested evaporation and condensation response
  • Identified wick failure within a closed system

Objective 5

[Insert official UAT objective text here]

Project C2 supports this objective by treating geometry as an engineering tool. Internal slopes, channels, vent placement, and surface features were used to guide environmental behavior without relying on powered hardware. The project depends on form driving function.

  • Used geometry to direct condensation routing
  • Designed passive environmental control features
  • Focused on system behavior driven by form

Objective 6

[Insert official UAT objective text here]

The project moves toward a minimum viable product by narrowing the design toward a repeatable, manufacturable lid system with clear functional goals, documented revisions, and a defined path for continued refinement. The current prototype is not final, but it establishes a realistic product direction.

  • Established a working prototype
  • Documented revision history and next steps
  • Focused on a scalable and repeatable product concept
